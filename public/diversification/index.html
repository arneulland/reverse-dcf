How it works

Each run draws ten years (or however many you choose) of quarterly returns. The market moves first, with 16% yearly volatility around the expected return for the scenario. Every stock follows the market and adds its own random swing. That swing is lopsided over time: a few stocks compound into giants while most lag, which is the pattern Bessembinder found in real markets. Your edge is added to the expected return of every stock you own. The index is the market itself. Each result is 2,500 runs; the chart uses 1,200 per point.

What it leaves out: fees and taxes, the fact that your picks may be correlated with each other (several software stocks move together), and that edge isn't steady from year to year. All three make concentration riskier than shown here.
